# Prototype exercise — how this works

Send this to every candidate alongside their brief, the Trestle API reference and
`fixtures.json`.

---

## What we're asking for

You'll get a brief describing a problem one of our customers has, in their own words. We'd
like you to build a working prototype that shows how you'd solve it, and then come and talk
us through it.

## Time

**Four to six hours, spread however suits you across the next week.**

We mean the time box. Going over it will not score better — we would genuinely rather see
what you chose to leave out and hear why. Nobody has ever been marked down here for an
unfinished feature that was deliberately cut.

## The platform

You're building against Trestle, our POS platform. You get the API reference and a file of
representative sandbox data. There is no live server — stub the API however you like. Treat
the reference as you would any third-party integration doc.

## Questions

**The customer is available and you should use them.** Email
[interview-client@…] and you'll get a reply within one business day, from the person in the
brief, answering as they would in real life.

Asking questions is expected. It is part of what we're looking at — both whether you ask
and what you ask. You will not be penalised for asking, and we won't treat a question as a
sign you couldn't work it out.

## AI tooling

Use whatever you normally use — Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot, your own prompt library, all
of it. We're not testing whether you can write code unaided; we're testing whether you can
take a vague problem from a customer and come back with something that works and that you
can defend. How you got there is your business.

## Your code stays yours

The prototype remains your property. We will not use it, keep it, or build on it. This is
an assessment, not unpaid work.

## What to send us, 24 hours before your session

Whatever you'd like us to have read — the code, a README, notes, the questions you asked
and what you did with the answers. Anything you want us to see before we sit down.

## The session — 45 minutes

| | |
|---|---|
| 10 min | Show us what you built, explained for someone who runs pubs, not someone who writes code |
| 15 min | Technical walkthrough — how it works, what you cut, what you'd do next |
| 15 min | We'll change a requirement and ask you to make the change while we watch |
| 5 min | Your questions for us |

Two of us will be there. One won't be technical.

The live change is not a trap and it's not a test of typing speed. We want to see how you
approach a change in code you know, out loud.
